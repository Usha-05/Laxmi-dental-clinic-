**Local treatment page copy**

| | |

| Suggested URL | /root-canal-treatment-vanasthalipuram/ or /treatments/root-canal |
| H1 | Root Canal Treatment in Vanasthalipuram |

Persistent tooth pain, pain while biting, sensitivity that lingers, swelling or a deeply decayed tooth may indicate irritation or infection inside the tooth. A root canal assessment helps determine whether the tooth can be preserved and what treatment is required.

### When should you book an assessment?
- Spontaneous or severe tooth pain
- Pain while chewing or biting
- Lingering sensitivity to hot or cold
- Swelling, gum boil or recurrent discharge near a tooth
- Deep decay, a fractured tooth or a large old filling

### How the tooth is evaluated
The dentist takes a history, examines the tooth and surrounding gums, and performs appropriate clinical tests. A dental X-ray is advised when required to evaluate the roots and surrounding bone. The findings, restorability of the tooth, treatment sequence and estimated cost are explained before starting.

### Typical treatment journey
1. Diagnosis and assessment of whether the tooth can be saved.
2. Local anaesthesia and isolation of the tooth.
3. Cleaning and shaping of the infected root-canal space.
4. Disinfection, filling of the canals and restoration of the tooth.
5. Assessment for a suitable final restoration or crown when indicated.

### Frequently asked questions
**Is every painful tooth treated with a root canal?**
No. Pain can arise from decay, gum problems, cracks, bite-related issues or other causes. Treatment is selected only after examination and appropriate tests.

**How many visits are required?**
The number of appointments depends on the tooth, infection, anatomy and clinical findings. The dentist explains the expected sequence after assessment.

**Is a crown always required?**
Not every tooth has the same requirement. The amount of remaining tooth structure, tooth position and bite influence the final restoration plan.

**Can antibiotics replace root canal treatment?**
Antibiotics do not remove infected tissue from inside a tooth. They are prescribed only when clinically indicated as part of an appropriate treatment plan.

A wisdom tooth may erupt normally, remain partly covered by gum, grow at an angle or remain trapped inside the jaw. Pain or swelling should be assessed to understand the tooth position, surrounding structures and whether removal is necessary.

### Common reasons for assessment
- Pain or swelling behind the last tooth
- Food trapping and repeated gum inflammation
- Difficulty opening the mouth or discomfort while chewing
- Decay in the wisdom tooth or adjacent tooth
- An impacted tooth seen on an X-ray

### What happens during assessment?
The surgeon examines the mouth and reviews a suitable dental X-ray or OPG when required. The tooth angle, root shape, available space and relationship to nearby structures are considered. The need for removal, expected complexity, aftercare and estimated cost are explained before the procedure.

### Aftercare overview
Written instructions should cover pressure on the gauze, food choices, oral hygiene, prescribed medicines, activity and warning signs. Healing varies according to the tooth position, procedure and individual health factors.

### Frequently asked questions
**Does every wisdom tooth need removal?**
No. Removal is advised according to symptoms, disease risk, tooth position and clinical findings.

**Why may an OPG be required?**
An OPG provides a panoramic view that can help assess wisdom teeth, roots, jawbone and nearby structures when clinically indicated.

**Is the cost the same for every wisdom tooth?**
No. Cost varies with tooth position, root anatomy, surrounding structures and procedure complexity. It should be explained after assessment.

**When should I seek urgent care after removal?**
Contact the clinic for worsening swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, fever, breathing or swallowing difficulty, or any concern listed in the surgeon's aftercare instructions.

| H1 | Teeth Cleaning and Gum Care in Vanasthalipuram |

Tartar cannot be removed completely by brushing at home. Professional cleaning helps remove deposits and surface stains, while a gum assessment identifies whether routine scaling is sufficient or deeper periodontal care may be required.

### Signs that need a dental check
- Visible tartar or persistent surface stains
- Bleeding while brushing or flossing
- Bad breath that keeps returning
- Swollen gums or gum tenderness
- Loose teeth or gum recession

### What the appointment may include
The dentist examines the teeth and gums, checks the distribution of deposits and looks for signs of gum disease. Scaling and polishing are performed when appropriate. Deep cleaning or further periodontal evaluation is recommended only when clinically required.

### Frequently asked questions
**Does scaling damage enamel?**
Professional scaling is designed to remove deposits from tooth surfaces. The dentist should explain the procedure and any existing sensitivity or gum recession.

**Why do deposits form again?**
Tartar can recur because plaque continuously forms. Oral hygiene technique, tooth position, saliva, diet, smoking and gum condition can influence recurrence.

**Will cleaning whiten my natural tooth colour?**
Cleaning removes deposits and some external stains. It does not change the intrinsic natural colour in the same way as a whitening procedure.

**How frequently should cleaning be done?**
The interval depends on gum health, deposit formation, oral hygiene and individual risk factors. A personalised recall interval is preferable to one fixed schedule for everyone.
